# Design

What the game is. Read this before gameplay work and update it when the design changes.

**Frameshift Bus.** The core loop below is the design. Built so far: the bus docks, charges and flies routes in the solar system (steps 1 to 4: docking, charging, flying and rerouting, without the planets' cost and time labels), with passengers, fuel, refuelling and the tow. What Play opens today is that solar system (Scenes and flow). The example game scene is still in the repo and shows the patterns to copy.

## Pitch

You drive a bus around a small solar system. Pick up passengers, plot a route through space and get them to their planet before they lose patience. The planets keep orbiting, so a route that is short now may not be short in a few seconds.

## Core loop

1. **Docked.** The bus starts docked at a planet. Passengers wait at planets, each with a destination planet, a fare and a patience timer. While docked (or charging), the player picks passengers up into the bus's seats.
2. **Set course.** Right-clicking a planet adds it to the end of the route. Each planet shows the fuel cost and travel time to it, both from the distance right now. Right-clicking empty space adds a point in space instead: with a planet on the route it is a **checkpoint**, which goes in just before the first planet on the route, so it bends the way to where the bus is headed rather than going on after it. There is one checkpoint before that planet: a new one replaces the one already there. With no planet on the route, the point replaces the one the bus is flying to, so a route in open space is one point, and right-clicking a planet then replaces that point with the planet. Clicking one planet after another builds a route of several stops; Backspace clears it, and in flight so does the control panel's Emergency stop. No modifier keys: Firefox opens its own context menu on Shift + right-click, and a page can't stop it. Left click is for selecting things, such as opening a planet. The route is drawn as a line from the bus through each point.
3. **Charge.** With a course set, the player holds the control panel's "Hold to frameshift" button down (experiment, below): the frameshift drive charges for 3 s while it is held, the bus turns toward its next stop meanwhile, and it leaves once charged. Letting go before then stops the charge and keeps the route. While it charges, the player can cancel, or change the route.
4. **Fly.** The bus flies the route point by point. The player can reroute at any time from wherever the bus is, the same way as setting a course, with no charge-up and no extra fuel cost: fuel burns by distance flown, so a reroute costs only the distance it adds. The same goes for a course set from a stop in space within 1 s of the bus coming to rest (`warm_time`), so a short stop costs nothing. After that the drive has gone cold, and a new course charges it for the full 3 s first, as at a planet, while the player holds the frameshift button: the ring fills, the bus turns toward its first stop and shakes, and the player can cancel, which leaves it holding in space.
5. **Arrive.** At a planet, passengers going there get off and pay by their mood: happy pays 1.2× the fare, okay 1×, annoyed 0.5×, furious nothing.
6. **Refuel.** For now every planet sells fuel, each at its own price. Docked at one, the player holds a button down in the dock panel, or in the planet's panel when they open it, to pump fuel in, paying as it flows. If the bus runs out of fuel in space, the player pays for a tow to the nearest planet. The tow costs money, and the time it takes costs passengers' patience.

**Control panel (experiment).** A wide, low strip at the bottom middle of the screen (`game/bus/bus_control_panel.tscn`), under the course hint, gathers the bus's status and its controls, and more of both go here later. Left to right: what the bus is doing with the seconds left to the next planet, over a track from the planet it left to that one; the fuel as a bar and a percentage (red at a quarter of a tank or less) over a person for each seat, filled while taken; and the frameshift button. Set off by holding the button down instead of having the drive charge by itself once a course is set, so leaving is the player's decision, and a route can be planned in full first. The button fills with the charge; held while passengers get on, it reads "Boarding..." and charges once they are on. While the bus flies under its own power it turns into a red "Emergency stop", which cancels the course on the press, so letting go of the hold that set the bus off never triggers it. The hold applies to every departure: from a planet, a planet stop along the route, and a stop in space once the drive is cold. It is behind `hold_to_charge` on the bus, on in the solar system, so turning it off brings back the charge that starts by itself (the button is then the stop while it charges too). Open questions: a key to hold as well as the button, and whether planet stops along a route should still leave by themselves.

**Fuel, as built.** A full tank holds 100 fuel and the bus burns 2.5 per unit flown, so a tank flies 40 units: about one trip from Ember to Frost and back, which are 12 to 23 units apart as they orbit. Only flying burns fuel; charging, parking and the float while docked don't. A bus can only be at a planet with an empty tank if something empties it there, such as the debug panel: it stays docked ("Out of fuel: refuel here"), a charge under way stops, and no course can be set until it refuels. Running dry: the moment the tank runs dry in flight, the route is cleared and the bus coasts to a stop ("Out of fuel: tow on its way"). A tow ship (`game/bus/tow_ship.tscn`: the bus's model with a blinking orange beacon on its roof) frameshifts in along the edge of the planet nearest to the bus, to a point just off it, and flies out to the bus, stopping just ahead of it, toward the planet. A pale cable joins its tail to the bus's nose, and it leads the bus there 0.4 units a second faster than that planet moves at the time, up to the bus's top speed, so the tow always catches its planet: planets move at 0.34 (Frost) to 0.47 (Ember) units a second, and the Moon at up to about 0.87 as it circles Terra. A tow is still slow, so riders lose patience on the way. It never goes closer to the planet than where it shifted in, so near the planet it moves round to the bus's side. Once the bus starts to park, the cable goes, and the ship veers off away from the bus and the planet and frameshifts out. A frameshift jump takes 0.3 s and 2.5 units: the ship stretches long and thin along its heading (squeezing back to its shape as it lands when it shifts in), and a thin white-cyan streak along the jump fades over half a second. The player can't set, change or cancel a course from running dry until the bus has docked. It docks there as usual (a stop, with passengers getting off), with a full tank, and the tow costs 20 credits, or all the player has when that is less. A full tank costs less than a tow at every planet, so refuelling is the cheap way to fill up.

**Refuelling, as built.** Every planet sells fuel at its own price (`fuel_price` on the planet, credits per unit of fuel; `sells_fuel` turns it off for a planet, and none has it off yet): a full tank from empty costs 6 credits at Giant, 8 at Frost, 10 at Ember, 12 at Terra and 15 at the Moon. The open planet's panel has a Fuel section under the waiting passengers: "Full tank 10 credits", the bus's fuel as a bar and a percentage (red at a quarter of a tank or less, as in the control panel, so it turns back to cyan as it fills), a "Hold to refuel" button, and a note under it. The note shows the player's credits, since the panel covers the counter, or why the button is off: "Dock here to refuel." when the bus isn't docked or charging at that planet, "Tank full." or "Not enough credits.". Holding the button down is the minigame: fuel flows from 12 a second, speeding up to 60 a second over 1.5 s, so a short tap tops up a little and a long hold fills fast; the button reads "Refuelling..." meanwhile. It stops when the player lets go, the tank is full or the credits run out. The dock panel has the same button, "Hold to fuel" with what filling the tank costs there, as "Hold to fuel (51 cr)", between its title and the passengers, so the player can refuel without opening the planet (`game/solar_system/fuel_button.tscn`). It is the tank's gauge too: a faint fill with the button's corners behind the label, as wide as the share of a full tank the bus holds, in the fuel's colour (red at a quarter of a tank or less), grows as fuel flows in. Held, the label morphs into the fuel's percentage (the passenger card's morph), which counts up as the tank fills, and morphs back when let go. When it is off, it reads why: "Tank full" or "Not enough credits". The bill is paid when the pump stops (let go, tank full, credits run out or the bus leaves), not as fuel flows: the whole credits it adds up to come off at once, in a red "-12" under the credits that pops, then the total counts down while it fades out drifting upward, as a fare's gain counts up. What is left under a credit carries over to the next refuel. It isn't there at a planet that sells no fuel, and the open planet's note reads "No fuel sold here.". Fuel is cheap next to fares for now (about 0.25 credits of fuel per unit flown, against fares of 3 per unit); tune both in playtests.

A passenger's patience holds until the bus leaves the planet they boarded at, then runs down while they ride, at half speed while the bus is docked. It holds while the bus parks at a planet. It starts at the direct trip time with some slack, so a straight trip keeps them happy and detours cost mood. Mood comes from patience left: happy at 60 % or more, okay at 30 % or more, annoyed above 0, furious at 0. The fare grows with the distance to the destination.

### On board

Each passenger on board has a card down the left edge of the screen: their portrait, name, destination (a small flat icon of the planet and its name), a patience bar in their mood's colour, their mood's name (it shakes a little when annoyed and more when furious, and holds still under Reduce motion; when the mood changes, the name fades out and back in as the new one while the bar blends into the new colour, Reduce motion or not), and a short line saying what they are doing ("Sleeping...", "Counting stars"), starting with "Finding their seat..." for a few seconds after boarding. When the line changes, it morphs: a wave sweeps left to right, the old letters drift up and blur away and the new ones rise into focus behind it, in about half a second. Under Reduce motion nothing moves: the old line fades out and the new one fades in on the spot. Built in `game/passengers/`, and running in the solar system:

- **Waiting and boarding (first phase).** Each planet, the Moon included, gets a new passenger every 8 to 20 s, up to 3 waiting, each going to another planet at random. Nobody new turns up at the planet the bus is docked at: its clock holds until the bus leaves, so waiting there gains nothing. They lose no patience while they wait. Once the bus has the waiting markers trait (Research, Traits, as built), under the name of each planet where someone waits, a small person and a number say how many, so the player can see who waits where while planning a route; they fade with the names while a planet is open. They also show in the open planet's panel (Controls). Whenever the bus docks at a planet, it makes a stop there: the passengers going there get off one at a time and pay. Nobody boards by themselves: while the bus is docked or charging, the dock panel sits in the bottom right. Its title is the planet's icon and name, and under it, where the planet sells fuel, the "Hold to fuel" button (Refuelling, as built), where the planet has a research tree a Research button, which opens the planet zoomed in on its Research tab (Research, Research tree), and a faint line. It lists who waits there, longest waiting first, each with their portrait, name, destination, fare and a Board button, or "Nobody waiting.", and under them a Board all button that picks up everyone that fits, longest waiting first. While every seat is taken or promised, it reads "Bus full 4/4" (the bus's capacity) and is off, as are the Board buttons. It stays in one place on screen, so the buttons never move under the pointer. The pointer over a passenger's row shows where they want to go (`game/solar_system/trip_arc.gd`): an arc from the planet to their destination, in their race's skin colour, rising off the orbit plane like a hop so it never reads as part of the flat route, and bowing sideways too where the trip points along the view, so it never looks straight. It is a line of rounded dashes with a thin dark outline, as wide on screen at any zoom, that leaves from the planet's edge and ends at a ring round the destination. It grows out from the planet in half a second, the ring closing in as it arrives; then the dashes flow toward the destination and the ring sends out a slow pulse. Under Reduce motion it fades in whole and holds still. A click on the row pins the arc, so it stays once the pointer leaves, until the row is clicked again, that passenger boards or the bus leaves; the row whose trip shows has a border in their race's colour. Both used to take the destination's icon colour, but Terra's green and the Giant's orange come too close to the races' and read as a race. The arc fades with the planets' names while a planet or the bus is open. It slides in from the right when the bus docks and back out when it leaves; a passenger picked up slides out of it to the right, cut off at its edge, and it grows and shrinks smoothly to fit. It is where whatever else the planet offers goes. Board picks a passenger up: they get on after anyone still getting off or picked up before them. The buttons are off while every one of the 4 seats is taken or promised to someone picked up. Getting off or on takes 0.6 s each, so a busy stop takes longer and an empty one none. The drive can't charge until the stop is over: a course set during it charges once the last passenger is on, and picking someone up while it charges holds the charge where it is until they are on. With a course set before docking, the bus waits docked for the player to hold the frameshift button (Control panel), so they can pick passengers up first; without `hold_to_charge`, the drive starts charging once everyone going there is off. A passenger getting off pays by the mood they arrived in. Their card shows their rating first (Rating, below), then slides out past the left edge of the screen and the cards below move up into the gap; one boarding slides in at the bottom from past the left edge. The game starts with the bus empty, docked at Ember, with 2 passengers waiting there and 1 at every other planet, so the first stop has someone to pick up and the first route somewhere to go; since Ember's clock holds while the bus is docked there, it would otherwise start with nobody.
- **Portraits.** A small photo on the left of the card (and of their row in a planet's panel while they wait), in a thin white frame tilted very slightly at random, of the passenger's alien head and shoulders over a blurred background in a deep shade of their race's colour, so the whole photo shows the race and the lighter skin stands out from it. It used to take the colours of the planet they boarded at, which said nothing the card needed and could hide a skin in its own colour. Each passenger has one of their race's two head shapes (`game/passengers/models/head_*.glb`; Research points, below), one of two eye styles (Googly, Bead) and their race's skin colour. The head and eyes are picked at random for now. Eye style is variety only, not a sign of race: at a card's size the eyes are a few pixels and already show the mood. The head sways and breathes slightly, the eyes blink and glance around (drawn in Godot, on markers in each model), and stalks wobble. Asleep (an idle line from the type's sleep lines, such as "Sleeping..."), the eyes drift shut to a curved line, the head nods forward and to one side and breathes slower, until a request wakes them. Their face shows their mood while they ride: annoyed, the lids slant down toward the middle of the face like a frown over smaller pupils; furious, more so, and a red cartoon anger mark (four short curved strokes with a dark outline) grows in at the head's upper right and pulses, and the head trembles. The skin never changes colour with mood, since it is what shows their race (Research points, below); an all-over red flush made every race read the same once furious. Under Reduce motion the mark holds still. Happy, they beam now and then, about as often as a few blinks: for a second or two, lower lids rise so the eyes smile into crescents, the pupils look up and the head bobs. A single eye in the middle of the face (the Blob) shows both off the eye instead, since any curve cut into one eye there reads as a mouth at a card's size: angry, a thick dark brow comes down over it, flat when annoyed and in a sharp V when furious, pushing the lid down below it with a strip of skin between; beaming, its shine turns into a four-pointed star, its pupil grows and pink blush ovals show on its cheeks. A beam rather than a constant half-smile, since a partly raised lid reads as a squint or a frown at a card's size, and a constant full grin would stop meaning anything. A portrait can also hold that joyful face, but nothing makes it yet. While a served snack or drink lasts, they eat or drink it in the photo. The aliens have no mouths, so the food is held up in front of where one would be, just below the eyes, and their eyes look down at it. The food always runs off the bottom of the photo, as if held from below, so it never floats. A snack is a bar held upright, its top bare and the rest in a pink wrapper: every 1.5 s it rises to the face, the head dips to bite, then munches. A drink is a tall white cup with a blue band under the rim and a short yellow straw up to the face: it stays put, and every couple of seconds the head dips to sip. The food rises into the photo from below when served and drops out again when it ends. Under Reduce motion, the food comes up and stays still. The need's data says which it is (`Need.served_action`), so an amenity serving it acts it out the same way. While they ride, the bust leans with the push felt on board: back as the bus speeds up, forward as it brakes (twice as far as sideways, and the head dips and comes closer, since a lean toward the camera barely shows), easing off as the bus glides in to park, and toward the outside of a turn; it sways back past upright when the push stops, and stalks trail behind. Waiting at a planet, they don't lean. Under Reduce motion only the eyes move. One offscreen view in the solar system renders every portrait on screen in a single pass (`PortraitStudio`, 12 slots), and each photo (`PassengerPhoto`) shows its own tile of it.
- **Trip time and fare** come from the straight distance at boarding: the trip time is the charge plus that distance at the bus's top speed, and the fare is 3 credits per unit times the type's multiplier. Planets move, so it is only an estimate.
- **Research points.** Every passenger is one of three races (`game/passengers/race.gd`), picked at random for now. A race shows in two ways, so it reads without colour too: its skin colour, and the build both its head shapes share, which differ in a detail so a race doesn't look the same every time. The portrait fits every head to its photo, so a build reads as a body type rather than a height. Getting off at their destination earns one research point of their race, whatever their rating. The points show in the resource bar (below): for each race a flask in its skin colour and how many the player has, which pops as it goes up. Researching an upgrade spends them (Research, Research tree). Each race's points are meant for one part of the bus: blue for its performance, orange for comfort on board, green for the stops.
  - **Blue** (long): tall, smooth heads with the eyes in the face. The Dome and the Bean, which has antennae.
  - **Orange** (short): low mounds with no neck, sitting straight on the shoulders. The Blob, with one big eye, and the Mound, wider and flatter with two eyes and two nubs on top.
  - **Green** (middle): a thin neck, with the eyes out on the ends of things. Stalks, eyes on two stalks, and the Hammer, eyes on the ends of a bar.
  - **Colours.** Blue `#6699ff`, orange `#fa8033` and green `#99e673`. With red-green colour blindness, orange and green both turn yellowish, and they stay apart only because the orange is darker and the green lighter, so their builds are the most different pair: no neck against a thin one. Blue stays blue. The planets' hues aren't final: the Giant (orange) and Terra (green) now come close to the races' colours. The photo backgrounds are deep shades of the same three: blue `#2e3d8c`, orange `#9e401a` and green `#296138`.
- **Credits** show in the resource bar, beside a dollar sign, with a comma between thousands (12,480). Payments at a stop collect in a green gain floating under the bar ("+15"): with each one it pops bigger for a moment and shows the new sum. Once everyone going there is off, after a short delay the total counts up to include it one credit at a time, while the gain fades out drifting upward.
- **Resource bar** (`game/solar_system/resource_bar.tscn`): a frosted-glass pill in the top right corner with the credits, the three research counts and the average rating, split by thin dividers, in Baloo 2 Bold with Lucide icons (`ui/fonts/`, `game/solar_system/icons/`). What is behind it shows blurred under a translucent fill. Every digit is the same width, so a count never makes it wobble: it only widens or narrows by a digit when a number gains or loses one (999 to 1,000). Its right edge stays put and its left edge glides to the new width in 0.3 s, easing out; under Reduce motion it changes at once. A count of the credits makes room for its widest number before it starts, and gives back what it no longer needs once it ends, so the pill changes width once per count rather than at each digit it passes (0 to 1,000 passes three); the total sits right-aligned in that room, so new digits appear on its left. So the start of a game doesn't move it either, each number always has room for what it shows early on: the credits for three digits (up to 999), each research count for two, and the rating for "0.0" while it shows a dash. The bar starts at that width, 284 px as in the design, and first changes when the credits reach 1,000 or a research count 100. Gains and losses float under the pill, taking no room in it, and fade out just under its edge as they drift up.
- **Rating.** A passenger getting off rates the ride in stars by the mood they arrived in: happy 5, okay 4, annoyed 2, furious 0. For now the mood is all that counts; later, what they could use on board (food, drinks, a bed, entertainment) adds to it. When the bus docks at their planet, everyone going there shows it on their card at once. The name and mood stay. The middle row, where they are going and their patience bar, sinks a few pixels and fades out while five empty stars drop in from just above and fade in, a moment behind it; then the stars fill up to their rating one after another, each swelling and settling as it turns yellow. The activity line morphs to "Getting off". Under Reduce motion the row only crossfades and the stars only change colour. The card stops following them and lingers for 2.2 s, counted from when the stop starts, long enough for five stars to fill and be read, and only after that and their turn to get off does it slide out. The player's average rating, the mean of every rating so far to one decimal, shows at the right end of the resource bar beside a yellow star, or a dash before the first. Like the credits, it waits for the stop: each rating pops a gain under the bar with how far the average has moved ("+0.2" in green, "-0.3" in red, "±0.0"), and once everyone going there is off, after a short delay the average moves to its new value while the gain fades out drifting upward. The very first rating has nothing to move from, so the average just appears with a pop. It changes nothing else yet (Open questions).
- **Bus status** shows in the control panel at the bottom middle (`game/bus/bus_control_panel.tscn`, Core loop, Control panel): what the bus is doing ("Docked", "Charging frameshift", "Travelling to Terra", "Docking to Terra", "Heading to waypoint", "Stopping", "Holding in space"), a track from the planet it left (or "Space") to the next planet on the route with the time left to it, and a fuel gauge (a pale cyan bar and the percentage left, both red at a quarter of a tank or less), a person for each seat, filled while taken, with the count ("3/4 seats"). Out of fuel, it reads "Out of fuel: tow on its way", then "Towed to Terra" once the tow ship has hitched on, and the frameshift button is off ("Under tow"). Docking is the braking at the end of a flight to a planet and the parking after it. The time left is an estimate from the distance now: the charge left, the route's straight legs at top speed, speeding up and braking. The track never moves back, so a planet that moves away slows it instead. Beside it, the frameshift button: held, it charges the drive, and letting go keeps a charging bus docked with its route; while the bus flies, it is the red Emergency stop, which slows it to a stop in space.

- **Needs.** Each passenger type has a few needs, such as a snack or a drink. Each need asks again a random while after the last one was served. While a request is open, the card says so ("Hungry. Wants a snack") and shows a button to serve it, and patience runs down several times faster. A request stays open until it is served, never expiring, since a request that expired would make ignoring a passenger cheaper than serving them. One request at a time, and the other needs' timers wait while it is open, so serving one doesn't set off the rest at once. A served need shows its own line ("Munching a snack") for a few seconds.
- **Amenities.** Something on the bus that serves one need by itself, the moment it is asked for, with its own line ("Getting a drink from the dispenser"). This is what upgrades buy: each one takes a chore off the player. None exist yet.
- **Kicking off.** The pointer over a passenger's card slides its options out from under the card to its right, in about a fifth of a second, and back under once the pointer leaves the card and them; under Reduce motion they only fade. For now there is one, a "Kick" button with a kicking shoe icon in front of the word (`game/passengers/kick_icon.svg`, from the design file's Kick Passenger icon). It kicks them off the bus at once, wherever it is, docked or in flight: their seat frees up, they pay nothing and rate nothing, and their card slides out past the left edge as when someone gets off, without the rating. Out of the bus's door, which is near the front of the side that faces the planet when docked, a small, soft human figure in a star jump, arms up and out and legs apart, all in their skin colour is thrown out and up, slowing as it goes and tumbling slowly, as if weightless, and after 4 s it shrinks away. It lets out a quiet Wilhelm scream as it is thrown, which fades out fast, as if it flew away from the bus (`game/passengers/wilhelm_scream.ogg`). Under Reduce motion it doesn't tumble. A card showing a rating, getting off, has no options. Built in `game/passengers/kicked_passenger.tscn`; the cabin's `kick()` takes them off.
- **Passenger types** are data (a `PassengerType` resource): fare multiplier, patience slack, needs, idle lines, sleep lines and names. There is one, the Commuter.

## Goal

A temporary goal while the loop is tuned:

- **Win:** deliver 30 passengers.
- **Lose:** the happiness score stays at 0 for 20 s.

The happiness score isn't defined yet (see Open questions).

## Look

Flat and cartoony, like a picture book of the solar system: solid colours with crisp edges, no textures, no realistic light. Everything is generated from shaders and meshes in code, so there are no image assets to make.

- **Camera.** Looks down at the orbit plane at an angle, with a narrow field of view (30°), so the system reads almost like a map but the planets still look round.
- **Planets** (`planet.gdshader`). Each is a sphere with up to three colour bands with hard edges: noise blobs for rocky planets (continents, craters), wobbly horizontal stripes for gas giants, and optional polar caps. The pattern turns with the planet's spin. Light is two tones only: a lit side in the planet's own colours, and a night side tinted by the same purple multiply on every planet, split by a line about a pixel wide at any zoom. The star's distance and the ambient light don't change a planet's colours.
- **Colours.** Soft, warm and saturated, never dark: sandy orange, leaf green, a pale lilac moon, an orange-striped gas giant with lilac rings, a pink ice planet with white caps. Planets are told apart by colour first, so each new one needs its own hue.
- **Star.** An unlit yellow sphere with a flat halo of a few see-through discs that always face the camera. No bloom or glow pass.
- **Rings and orbits.** Unlit, flat bands with hard edges. Every orbit is drawn as a thin, faint lilac line, so the player can see where each planet is going.
- **Planet icons** (`planet_icon.gdshader`). The UI shows a planet as a flat disc in its one icon colour, with the same purple night side as in the world, lit from the upper left. A planet with rings shows them as a line at its tilt, passing behind the disc at the top and in front at the bottom; its disc is smaller, so the rings fit at any tilt. Each planet's icon colour is set by hand to the colour it reads as from the camera.
- **Names.** Each planet and moon has its name just under it: small off-white text with the sky's lilac, a little see-through, with a thin navy outline so it still reads where it crosses an orbit line. The text faces the camera and keeps its size on screen at any zoom; the bus passes in front of it.
- **Sky** (`SpaceSky`). A deep navy-violet background with faint coloured fog and two layers of small stars. It is dim on purpose, so the planets stand out. It doesn't light the planets.

- **Bus** (`game/bus/models/bus.glb`, from the model library). A flat yellow space bus with rounded, faceted edges: a sloped windscreen, a row of dark lilac side windows over a white stripe, a door, a white roof with two vents, a dark hull underneath (no wheels) and two thrusters at the back. It is lit with the planets' two tones in the colours it was made with. The thrusters are dark while it is docked. As the drive charges, a pale cyan exhaust cone grows out of each one, full length in flight, and shrinks back after it docks. In flight the bus leaves a flat pale cyan trail on the orbit plane that narrows and fades out over 2.5 s. Under the pointer it has a white border as wide on screen as a planet's: only past its silhouette, never over its own roof, vents or nozzles (`bus_outline.gdshader`, drawn where the bus hasn't marked the stencil).

Anything new (passengers, UI) follows the same rules: flat colour, hard edges, a two-tone shade at most, no textures. It keeps the scene cheap on the web too: no post-processing so far, since each effect adds a full-screen pass (`docs/web-constraints.md`).

## Controls

Planned for the core loop, not built yet: the open planet's panel will also show the fuel cost and time to get there.

Esc stays pause only. In browser fullscreen the browser takes Esc to leave fullscreen and the game never sees it, so Esc can't close a panel the same way in a window and in fullscreen.

Built, in the solar system, which Play opens:

| Input | Action |

The bus flies on the orbit plane at 1.2 units a second, a little faster than the mockup's trip times: a planet orbits at under 40 % of the bus's speed, so the bus catches one without a long chase. Docked, it floats beside its planet (a barely visible bob and sway) and moves with it. Leaving a planet charges the drive first, while the player holds the frameshift button (Core loop, Control panel): a white ring round the bus fills clockwise (temporary), and the bus shakes slightly, unless Reduce motion is on. While it charges, the bus turns toward its next stop, so it leaves already facing it, and speeds up as it goes. Flying to a planet, it slows down as it nears it, then parks: it glides the last half unit in close to the planet and swings side-on, with its door toward it, so passengers get off from the side. Passengers' patience holds while it parks, and it has docked (the bell rings and the stop starts) only once it is parked. A course set while it parks charges once it has. Charging, it pulls out a little from the planet as it turns, so its tail clears it. It passes through points in space, docks at every planet on the route and charges again before leaving each one, and holds still at a point in space that ends the route. A course set from there within 1 s of stopping leaves at once; later, the drive has cooled and charges first. A planet stop is the planet's near side, so the bus chases a moving planet. Right-clicking the planet it is docked at adds it as the last stop, so a route can end where it started; with no route, it does nothing. Until the player first sets a course, a hint at the bottom of the screen says how: "Right-click planets or space to add stops. Backspace clears the route." The route is a flat orange line from the bus through each stop, with a diamond at each point in space. It runs to a planet's surface, and every planet on the route has an orange border, so planets read as part of the route. Under the pointer, the last stop's diamond turns white, as a planet's border does, and right-clicking it removes it. Diamonds earlier on the route stay orange.

**Opening a planet.** The camera blends in to the planet, from the same angle, and follows it along its orbit. A bigger planet fills more of the screen's height, from about a third for the Moon to half for the Giant, so sizes still compare; and the camera stays far enough back that rings fit across the space beside the panel. The rings are why the Giant can't come closer: they are 2.2 times its radius. It sits in the middle of the space left of the panel, which slides in down the right side of the screen in step with the camera, arriving as the zoom completes (`game/solar_system/planet_panel.tscn`): the planet's icon and name, a row for each passenger waiting there, longest waiting first, with their portrait, their name over their destination, and their fare, kept up to date as they arrive and board, and under them the Fuel section (Core loop, Refuelling, as built). A passenger who turns up while the panel is open slides in from its right edge; the rows already there stay as they are. The panel covers the credits and the dock panel while open, the course hint hides, and the control panel keeps to the space left of it as it slides in and out. The planets' names fade out, and the open planet's white hover border fades out and stays off while it is open, so nothing sits on the close-up; both fade back in when it closes. Pan and zoom do nothing while a planet is open; closing it blends the camera back to where it was. Right click still sets the course as usual.

**Opening the bus.** Works like opening a planet: the camera blends in to the bus and follows it, docked or in flight, framed in the space left of the bus panel (`game/bus/bus_panel.tscn`), which slides in down the right side the same way. The panel is titled "Frameshift Bus" and lists the bus's stats (Research, Stats, as built), one row each: its name, its value, and once upgrades change it, how much they add in all in green ("Top speed 1.44 +20%"), or take away in red ("Fuel tank 80 -20%"). Seats show in whole seats, and what they add as seats ("Seats 5 +1"). Under them, a Traits section names each trait the bus has ("Fuel forecast"), or reads "None yet.". Rows update as modifiers and traits come and go. Only one of the bus and a planet is open at a time.

WASD reads physical key positions, so it sits in the same place on every keyboard layout. Edge pan starts only once the pointer has moved inside the game, and stops when it leaves the window or the window loses focus, so a pointer that left through an edge doesn't keep the camera moving. In the example game scene, a left click on the board or a piece's top places a piece.

## Scenes and flow

```
main menu ──Play──▶ solar system ──Esc──▶ pause menu ──Main menu──▶ main menu
    │                                          │
    └──Settings──▶ settings panel ◀──────────Settings
```

- **Main menu** (`ui/main_menu/`): the main scene. Title, Play, Settings, Quit (Quit hidden on web). On web, a title card (game title, "Click to start") comes first, and that click starts the menu music.
- **Example game** (`game/game.tscn`): no longer reached from the menu; its tests still load it. The camera with one Phantom Camera shot (`BoardShot`), one shadowed sun, the soft-look environment, the board, and the HUD (`game/hud.tscn`) with the placed count. Placing a piece emits `Events.piece_placed`, and the HUD listens. Placing a piece calls `Feedback.shake`, which does nothing when Reduce motion is on. It instances the pause menu.
- **Pause menu** (`ui/pause_menu/`): Resume, Settings, Main menu. Pauses the tree; music keeps playing. Also opens when the player leaves browser fullscreen.
- **Settings panel** (`ui/settings/`): Master, Music and SFX volume, Reduce motion, and Edge pan (on by default; off, only the keys pan the camera). Shared by the main menu and the pause menu.
- **Solar system** (`game/solar_system/`): what Play opens. A yellow star and four flat, toon-lit planets (one with a moon, one with rings) on circular orbits, over the `SpaceSky` with parallax on (its star and fog layers drift slightly as the camera moves, and hold still under Reduce motion), seen through an RTS camera rig (`camera_rig.tscn`): one Phantom Camera shot that pans and zooms within limits set on the rig. The scene's script turns the pointer into planet hover, opening planets and course clicks for the bus (`game/bus/bus.tscn`), which starts docked at Ember. It also runs the passengers (a `Cabin`, `WaitingPassengers`, and the HUD with the passenger cards and the resource bar). It instances the pause menu.

Scene changes go through `SceneFlow.go_to`, which fades to a black loading card ("Loading…" over a bar) while the next scene loads, then back in. The bar counts the real work in steps, so it moves on the single-threaded web build too: loading the scene's files one by one, then drawing the scene the first time one new material a frame, which compiles their shaders and is most of the wait on the web.

## Scope

- **In:** the core loop, the temporary goal, one kind of passenger, fuel and depots. Already built: the scenes above, ambient menu music, UI and placement SFX, settings saved across sessions.
- **Out for now:** everything under Research until it is designed (the research tree is designed: Research, Research tree), saving progress, dialogue, positional or adaptive audio.

## Research

Features we will very probably build, but that need more design work first.

- **Research tree.** Upgrades for the bus, bought with credits and research points. Each race gives its own kind of research point (Passengers, Research points, as built), so an upgrade can ask for points of one race.
  - **Trees, as designed.** Each planet can have a research tree of its own: the upgrades it offers, linked by what each needs first. An upgrade has a cost in credits and research points, usually of the one race whose points are for the part of the bus it improves (Passengers, Research points), so a planet's tree can send the player looking for that race; the upgrades that must be researched before it, all of them, from the same planet's tree; and what it does once researched: modifiers on the bus's stats (Stats, as built), traits (Traits, as built), or both. Each upgrade is researched once, and a bigger step of the same thing is its own upgrade after it in the tree (Tank I, then Tank II). Researching happens only while the bus is docked or charging at that planet, as refuelling does, so routing to a planet is how the player gets at its tree. An upgrade is in one of five states:
    - **Hidden:** a "?" in place of its icon, with "???" for its name and description and no cost. Only an upgrade marked hidden is, and only until everything it needs is researched; then it shows like any other.
    - **Locked:** shown dimmed; the lines to it show what it needs.
    - **Available:** everything it needs is researched and the player can pay for it.
    - **Can't afford:** available but for the cost, which shows in red.
    - **Researched:** filled in, its effects on the bus.
  - **In the planet panel, as built.** The open planet's panel has two tabs: **Services**, the waiting passengers and Fuel (Opening a planet), and where anything else a planet offers goes; and **Research**, only at a planet with a tree (`research_tree` on the planet). With one tab, there is no tab bar. It opens on Services; opening another planet straight from an open one keeps the Research tab when the new planet has one. The dock panel's Research button opens the docked planet straight on its Research tab. The Research tab draws the tree top to bottom (`game/research/research_tree_view.tscn`): a row for each tier, each upgrade a square tile in its column showing only its icon (`icon` on the upgrade: a white Lucide icon, like the resource bar's, from `game/research/icons/`; a tile without one shows its title's first letter), and a line from each upgrade up to each one it requires, faint until that one is researched, then in its race's skin colour. A tile shows one of three looks, in its upgrade's race's skin colour as the accent (the race whose points it costs most, `Upgrade.get_race()`; an upgrade that costs no points takes cyan): **available** (everything it needs is researched, whether or not the player can pay yet) is a dark tile with an accent border and a white icon; **researched** is filled with the accent, the icon in the race's deep photo-background colour; **not available** (locked or hidden) is a flat, dim tile with a dim icon, a hidden one showing "?" in place of its icon. The pointer over any upgrade shows its card just under it (`upgrade_card.tscn`), or above it where there's no room: its name, a short line on what it does, and its cost with the resource bar's icons and font (the gold dollar sign and the credits, then a flask in each race's colour and its points), with what the player lacks in red. A hidden one shows "???" for its name and description and no cost, and a researched one no cost. A click on an available upgrade researches it at once (`ResearchLab`): the credits come off as a red loss under the credits, the points come off their counts, and its modifiers and traits go on the bus. Away from that planet, the whole tree draws at half strength, the cards still show, and "Dock here to research." reads over it. The planet panel and the bus panel are both 480 pixels wide, so switching between them never changes it.
  - **Trees, as built.** Three planets have a tree (`game/research/trees/`), each in one race's colour and paid for with that race's points, and the rest have none. They branch and join like talent trees rather than running in separate columns: an early upgrade opens more than one, some need upgrades from two branches, and each ends in a capstone that needs two branches and is hidden until both are researched. Costs go by tier, the same in every tree for now: 20 credits and 2 points in the top row, then 45 and 4, 90 and 7, 150 and 10, and 250 and 14. Only the upgrades on a stat do anything yet; the rest read "NOT IMPLEMENTED" on their card and change nothing when researched. "A ← B" below means A needs B.
    - **Blue, at Frost** (the bus's performance), five rows: Speed 1 and Fuel tank 1 at the top. Speed 2 ← Speed 1, Frameshift 1 ← Speed 1 and Fuel tank 1, Fuel forecast ← Fuel tank 1 (the fuel forecast trait), Fuel tank 2 ← Fuel tank 1. Speed 3, Frameshift 2 and Fuel tank 3 each ← the one above. Extra insulation ← Frameshift 2 and Fuel tank 3. Capstone: Sunscreen ← Speed 3 and Extra insulation. Speed is +10 % top speed a step, Fuel tank +25 % fuel tank a step; Frameshift (a shorter charge), Extra insulation and Sunscreen aren't implemented.
    - **Green, at Terra** (the stops), a diamond: Door chime, then Station cameras and Station waiting lounge ← Door chime, and the capstone Mind reading machine ← both. Only Door chime is implemented: it gives the waiting markers trait.
    - **Orange, at Giant** (comfort on board), four rows: Extra seat, Food dispenser 1 and Gamepads at the top. Four Extra seats down the left, each ← the one above (+1 seat each). Pillows ← the first Extra seat and Food dispenser 1, Sleeping pods ← the second Extra seat and Pillows. Food dispenser 2 ← Food dispenser 1, Entertainment system ← Gamepads, and the capstone Catering robot ← Food dispenser 2 and Entertainment system. Only the seats are implemented.
    - The icons are Lucide's: gauge, zap, fuel, route (Fuel forecast), thermometer-snowflake and sun; bell-ring, cctv, brain and sofa; armchair, sandwich, bot, bed-single, bed-double, gamepad-2 and tv. A chain repeats its icon. The debug panel's trait toggles take a trait away even when an upgrade gave it, while the upgrade still reads "Researched".
  - **Stats, as built.** An upgrade that makes the bus better at something, rather than adding an amenity, is a modifier on one of the bus's stats (`game/bus/stat.gd`). A stat is a base value with modifiers on top, each flat or a percent: the flat ones add to the base, then the percents add together and scale the sum, so a base of 8 with +5, +10 % and +20 % is (8 + 5) × 1.3, in any order. Saves will store which upgrades were bought and apply their modifiers again on load. The stats so far: top speed (base 1.2), fuel tank (base 100, the fuel a full tank holds) and seats (base 4, rounded to the nearest whole seat). Each has a floor however much modifiers take away (0.1 top speed, 1 fuel, 1 seat), so the bus always moves, holds fuel and has a seat. A bigger tank's new room starts empty, and a smaller one spills what no longer fits. Fewer seats than riders leaves everyone on board, someone already picked up still gets on, and nobody else boards until enough have got off. Passengers' patience and fares still come from the base top speed, so a faster bus makes for happier passengers rather than less patient ones.
  - **Traits, as built.** An upgrade that unlocks a feature, rather than making a number bigger, gives the bus a trait (`game/bus/bus_traits.gd`): the bus either has it or doesn't, and the feature shows or works only while it does. Two upgrades can give the same trait; it stays until both take it back. There are two so far, each sold by an upgrade (Research tree, Trees, as built): Door chime in the green tree gives the waiting markers, and Fuel forecast in the blue tree the fuel forecast. The debug panel's Traits card turns each on too. **Waiting markers** show how many wait at each planet, under its name (Passengers, Waiting and boarding); without them, the player finds out by opening a planet. The **fuel forecast** (`game/solar_system/fuel_forecast.tscn`): over the route's last stop, just above the planet or the diamond, how much of the tank the whole course burns ("-22% fuel"), in the planets' names' off-white, or in low fuel's red when the bus hasn't that much left. It counts the straight legs from the bus through each stop as everything is now, short of each planet by where the bus starts to park (parking burns none), so with planets moving it is an estimate. It hides with no course and during a tow, and fades with the planets' names while a planet is open.
- **Passenger types.** Around three types with different qualities. The prototype had Business (pays 1.6×, little patience), Tourist (pays 1×, lots of patience) and Student (pays 0.7×, in between). Types also differ in what they need on board, so a rich passenger might want a sleeping pod where a student wants only snacks.
- **Environment.** How space affects the bus and passengers. A hot star makes passengers on a route near it hot; a big planet's gravity slows the bus down near it.

### Open questions

- **Happiness score.** What it measures. One option: the average mood of the passengers on board, with the 20 s countdown paused when the bus is empty, so a bus full of furious passengers is the losing state.
- **What the rating does.** It is only shown so far. Options: it sets how often passengers turn up or which types do, it gates upgrades, it is the happiness score of the goal, or it is the leaderboard score. Also whether a run's average should weigh recent rides more, since one ride barely moves the mean of fifty.
- **Tow.** Set for now at 20 credits, or all the player has, and 0.4 units a second faster than the planet it goes to (Core loop, Fuel, as built). Tune both in playtests. It shows as a red "-20" under the credits, as refuelling's bill does.
- **Fuel shown for a free point and for checkpoints.** Whether the route line shows the total fuel and time like a planet does.
- **Passenger cards while a planet is open.** They stay down the left edge and can cover part of the open planet. Hide them, shrink them, or leave them?
- **Boarding with the docked planet open.** The planet panel covers the dock panel, and its rows have no Board button, so the player closes it to pick anyone up. Add Board buttons to the planet panel while the bus is docked there, or leave it?
- **Research points and rating.** A delivery earns its point whatever the rating, so a furious passenger is worth as much research as a happy one. Whether the rating should scale it (say, a point only from 3 stars up), or stay out of research and only count for credits.
- **Race and type.** Race and passenger type are separate for now: any race can be any type. Whether they stay independent, or a race leans toward some types or some planets, so the player can hunt for one kind of point.
- **Seats and waiting passengers.** The prototype had 4 seats and up to 3 waiting per planet; keep them until playtests say otherwise.
