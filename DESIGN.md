# Design

What the game is. Read this before gameplay work and update it when the design changes.

**Frameshift Bus.** The core loop below is the design. Built so far: the bus docks, charges and flies routes in the solar system (steps 1 to 4: docking, charging, flying and rerouting, without the planets' cost and time labels), with passengers, fuel, refuelling and the tow. What Play opens today is that solar system (Scenes and flow). The example game scene is still in the repo and shows the patterns to copy.

## Pitch

You drive a bus around a small solar system. Pick up passengers, plot a route through space and get them to their planet before they lose patience. The planets keep orbiting, so a route that is short now may not be short in a few seconds.

## Core loop

1. **Docked.** The bus starts docked at a planet. Passengers wait at planets, each with a destination planet, a fare and a patience timer. While docked (or charging), the player picks passengers up into the bus's seats.
2. **Set course.** Right-clicking a planet adds it to the end of the route. Each planet shows the fuel cost and travel time to it, both from the distance right now. Right-clicking empty space adds a point in space instead. Clicking one stop after another builds a route of several points; Backspace, or the bus status panel's cancel button, clears it. No modifier keys: Firefox opens its own context menu on Shift + right-click, and a page can't stop it. Left click is for selecting things, such as opening a planet. The route is drawn as a line from the bus through each point.
3. **Charge.** The frameshift drive charges for 3 s before the bus leaves a planet, and the bus turns toward its next stop meanwhile. While it charges, the player can cancel, or change the route.
4. **Fly.** The bus flies the route point by point. The player can reroute at any time from wherever the bus is, the same way as setting a course, with no charge-up and no extra fuel cost: fuel burns by distance flown, so a reroute costs only the distance it adds.
5. **Arrive.** At a planet, passengers going there get off and pay by their mood: happy pays 1.2× the fare, okay 1×, annoyed 0.5×, furious nothing.
6. **Refuel.** For now every planet sells fuel, each at its own price. Docked at one, the player holds a button down in the dock panel, or in the planet's panel when they open it, to pump fuel in, paying as it flows. If the bus runs out of fuel in space, the player pays for a tow to the nearest planet. The tow costs money, and the time it takes costs passengers' patience.

**Fuel, as built.** A full tank holds 100 fuel and the bus burns 2.5 per unit flown, so a tank flies 40 units: about one trip from Ember to Frost and back, which are 12 to 23 units apart as they orbit. Only flying burns fuel; charging, parking and the float while docked don't. A bus can only be at a planet with an empty tank if something empties it there, such as the debug panel: it stays docked ("Out of fuel: refuel here"), a charge under way stops, and no course can be set until it refuels. Running dry: the moment the tank runs dry in flight, the route is cleared and the bus coasts to a stop ("Out of fuel: tow on its way"). A tow ship (`game/bus/tow_ship.tscn`: the bus's model with a blinking orange beacon on its roof) frameshifts in along the edge of the planet nearest to the bus, to a point just off it, and flies out to the bus, stopping just ahead of it, toward the planet. A pale cable joins its tail to the bus's nose, and it leads the bus there 0.4 units a second faster than that planet moves at the time, up to the bus's top speed, so the tow always catches its planet: planets move at 0.34 (Frost) to 0.47 (Ember) units a second, and the Moon at up to about 0.87 as it circles Terra. A tow is still slow, so riders lose patience on the way. It never goes closer to the planet than where it shifted in, so near the planet it moves round to the bus's side. Once the bus starts to park, the cable goes, and the ship veers off away from the bus and the planet and frameshifts out. A frameshift jump takes 0.3 s and 2.5 units: the ship stretches long and thin along its heading (squeezing back to its shape as it lands when it shifts in), and a thin white-cyan streak along the jump fades over half a second. The player can't set, change or cancel a course from running dry until the bus has docked. It docks there as usual (a stop, with passengers getting off), with a full tank, and the tow costs 20 credits, or all the player has when that is less. A full tank costs less than a tow at every planet, so refuelling is the cheap way to fill up.

**Refuelling, as built.** Every planet sells fuel at its own price (`fuel_price` on the planet, credits per unit of fuel; `sells_fuel` turns it off for a planet, and none has it off yet): a full tank from empty costs 6 credits at Giant, 8 at Frost, 10 at Ember, 12 at Terra and 15 at the Moon. The open planet's panel has a Fuel section under the waiting passengers: "Full tank 10 credits", the bus's fuel as a bar and a percentage (red at a quarter of a tank or less, as in the bus status, so it turns back to cyan as it fills), a "Hold to refuel" button, and a note under it. The note shows the player's credits, since the panel covers the counter, or why the button is off: "Dock here to refuel." when the bus isn't docked or charging at that planet, "Tank full." or "Not enough credits.". Holding the button down is the minigame: fuel flows from 12 a second, speeding up to 60 a second over 1.5 s, so a short tap tops up a little and a long hold fills fast; the button reads "Refuelling..." meanwhile. Credits go a whole credit at a time as the fuel adds up to one, and what is left under a credit carries over to the next refuel. It stops when the player lets go, the tank is full or the credits run out. The dock panel has the same button, "Hold to fuel" with what filling the tank costs there, as "Hold to fuel (51 cr)", between its title and the passengers, so the player can refuel without opening the planet (`game/solar_system/fuel_button.tscn`). It has round corners, as the cards' rows do. It is the tank's gauge too: a faint fill with the same corners behind the label, as wide as the share of a full tank the bus holds, in the fuel's colour (red at a quarter of a tank or less), grows as fuel flows in. Held, the label morphs into the fuel's percentage (the passenger card's morph), which counts up as the tank fills, and morphs back when let go. When it is off, it reads why: "Tank full" or "Not enough credits". It isn't there at a planet that sells no fuel, and the open planet's note reads "No fuel sold here.". Fuel is cheap next to fares for now (about 0.25 credits of fuel per unit flown, against fares of 3 per unit); tune both in playtests.

A passenger's patience holds until the bus leaves the planet they boarded at, then runs down while they ride, at half speed while the bus is docked. It holds while the bus parks at a planet. It starts at the direct trip time with some slack, so a straight trip keeps them happy and detours cost mood. Mood comes from patience left: happy at 60 % or more, okay at 30 % or more, annoyed above 0, furious at 0. The fare grows with the distance to the destination.

### On board

Each passenger on board has a card down the left edge of the screen: their portrait, name, destination (a small flat icon of the planet and its name), a patience bar in their mood's colour, their mood's name (it shakes a little when annoyed and more when furious, and holds still under Reduce motion; when the mood changes, the name fades out and back in as the new one while the bar blends into the new colour, Reduce motion or not), and a short line saying what they are doing ("Sleeping...", "Counting stars"), starting with "Finding their seat..." for a few seconds after boarding. When the line changes, it morphs: a wave sweeps left to right, the old letters drift up and blur away and the new ones rise into focus behind it, in about half a second. Under Reduce motion nothing moves: the old line fades out and the new one fades in on the spot. Built in `game/passengers/`, and running in the solar system:

- **Waiting and boarding (first phase).** Each planet, the Moon included, gets a new passenger every 8 to 20 s, up to 3 waiting, each going to another planet at random. Nobody new turns up at the planet the bus is docked at: its clock holds until the bus leaves, so waiting there gains nothing. They lose no patience while they wait. Once the bus has the waiting markers trait (Research, Traits, as built), under the name of each planet where someone waits, a small person and a number say how many, so the player can see who waits where while planning a route; they fade with the names while a planet is open. They also show in the open planet's panel (Controls). Whenever the bus docks at a planet, it makes a stop there: the passengers going there get off one at a time and pay. Nobody boards by themselves: while the bus is docked or charging, the dock panel sits just above the bus status in the bottom right. Its title is the planet's icon and name, and under it the "Hold to fuel" button where the planet sells fuel (Refuelling, as built). It lists who waits there, longest waiting first, each with their portrait, name, destination, fare and a Board button, or "Nobody waiting.", and under them a Board all button that picks up everyone that fits, longest waiting first. While every seat is taken or promised, it reads "Bus full 4/4" (the bus's capacity) and is off, as are the Board buttons. It stays in one place on screen, so the buttons never move under the pointer. The pointer over a passenger's row shows where they want to go (`game/solar_system/trip_arc.gd`): an arc from the planet to their destination, in the destination's icon colour, rising off the orbit plane like a hop so it never reads as part of the flat route, and bowing sideways too where the trip points along the view, so it never looks straight. It is a line of rounded dashes with a thin dark outline, as wide on screen at any zoom, that leaves from the planet's edge and ends at a ring round the destination. It grows out from the planet in half a second, the ring closing in as it arrives; then the dashes flow toward the destination and the ring sends out a slow pulse. Under Reduce motion it fades in whole and holds still. A click on the row pins the arc, so it stays once the pointer leaves, until the row is clicked again, that passenger boards or the bus leaves; the row whose trip shows has a border in the destination's colour. The arc fades with the planets' names while a planet or the bus is open. It slides in from the right when the bus docks and back out when it leaves; a passenger picked up slides out of it to the right, cut off at its edge, and it grows and shrinks smoothly to fit. It is where whatever else the planet offers goes. Board picks a passenger up: they get on after anyone still getting off or picked up before them. The buttons are off while every one of the 4 seats is taken or promised to someone picked up. Getting off or on takes 0.6 s each, so a busy stop takes longer and an empty one none. The drive can't charge until the stop is over: a course set during it charges once the last passenger is on, and picking someone up while it charges holds the charge where it is until they are on. With a course set before docking, the drive starts charging once everyone going there is off, so the player has the 3 s charge to pick passengers up, or longer by picking them one after another. A passenger getting off pays by the mood they arrived in. Their card slides out past the left edge of the screen and the cards below move up into the gap; one boarding slides in at the bottom from past the left edge. The game starts with the bus empty, docked at Ember.
- **Portraits.** A small photo on the left of the card (and of their row in a planet's panel while they wait), in a thin white frame tilted very slightly at random, of the passenger's alien head and shoulders over a blurred background in the colours of the planet they boarded at. Each passenger is one of five head shapes (`game/passengers/models/head_*.glb`: Dome, Blob, Stalks, Bean, Hammer), one of two eye styles (Googly, Bead) and one of three race colours (teal, coral, mustard), all random for now. The head sways and breathes slightly, the eyes blink and glance around (drawn in Godot, on markers in each model), and stalks wobble. Asleep (an idle line from the type's sleep lines, such as "Sleeping..."), the eyes drift shut to a curved line, the head nods forward and to one side and breathes slower, until a request wakes them. While they ride, the bust leans with the push felt on board: back as the bus speeds up, forward as it brakes (twice as far as sideways, and the head dips and comes closer, since a lean toward the camera barely shows), easing off as the bus glides in to park, and toward the outside of a turn; it sways back past upright when the push stops, and stalks trail behind. Waiting at a planet, they don't lean. Under Reduce motion only the eyes move. One offscreen view in the solar system renders every portrait on screen in a single pass (`PortraitStudio`, 12 slots), and each photo (`PassengerPhoto`) shows its own tile of it.
- **Trip time and fare** come from the straight distance at boarding: the trip time is the charge plus that distance at the bus's top speed, and the fare is 3 credits per unit times the type's multiplier. Planets move, so it is only an estimate.
- **Credits** show in the top right. Payments at a stop collect in a green gain just under the total ("+15"): with each one it pops bigger for a moment and shows the new sum. Once everyone going there is off, after a short delay the total counts up to include it one credit at a time, while the gain fades out drifting upward.
- **Bus status** shows in the bottom right (`game/bus/bus_status_panel.tscn`): what the bus is doing ("Docked", "Charging frameshift", "Travelling to Terra", "Docking to Terra", "Heading to waypoint", "Stopping", "Holding in space"), a track from the planet it left (or "Space") to the next planet on the route with the time left to it, and a fuel gauge (a pale cyan bar and the percentage left, both red at a quarter of a tank or less), a person for each seat, filled while taken, with the count ("3/4 seats"). Out of fuel, it reads "Out of fuel: tow on its way", then "Towed to Terra" once the tow ship has hitched on, and the cancel button hides. Docking is the braking at the end of a flight to a planet and the parking after it. The time left is an estimate from the distance now: the charge left, the route's straight legs at top speed, speeding up and braking. The track never moves back, so a planet that moves away slows it instead. While the bus charges or flies, a button cancels the course: "Cancel" keeps a charging bus docked, and "Cancel route" slows a flying one to a stop in space.

- **Needs.** Each passenger type has a few needs, such as a snack or a drink. Each need asks again a random while after the last one was served. While a request is open, the card says so ("Hungry. Wants a snack") and shows a button to serve it, and patience runs down several times faster. A request stays open until it is served, never expiring, since a request that expired would make ignoring a passenger cheaper than serving them. One request at a time, and the other needs' timers wait while it is open, so serving one doesn't set off the rest at once. A served need shows its own line ("Munching a snack") for a few seconds.
- **Amenities.** Something on the bus that serves one need by itself, the moment it is asked for, with its own line ("Getting a drink from the dispenser"). This is what upgrades buy: each one takes a chore off the player. None exist yet.
- **Passenger types** are data (a `PassengerType` resource): fare multiplier, patience slack, needs, idle lines, sleep lines and names. There is one, the Commuter.

## Goal

A temporary goal while the loop is tuned:

- **Win:** deliver 30 passengers.
- **Lose:** the happiness score stays at 0 for 20 s.

The happiness score isn't defined yet (see Open questions).

## Look

Flat and cartoony, like a picture book of the solar system: solid colours with crisp edges, no textures, no realistic light. Everything is generated from shaders and meshes in code, so there are no image assets to make.

- **Camera.** Looks down at the orbit plane at an angle, with a narrow field of view (30°), so the system reads almost like a map but the planets still look round.
- **Planets** (`planet.gdshader`). Each is a sphere with up to three colour bands with hard edges: noise blobs for rocky planets (continents, craters), wobbly horizontal stripes for gas giants, and optional polar caps. The pattern turns with the planet's spin. Light is two tones only: a lit side in the planet's own colours, and a night side tinted by the same purple multiply on every planet, split by a line about a pixel wide at any zoom. The star's distance and the ambient light don't change a planet's colours.
- **Colours.** Soft, warm and saturated, never dark: sandy orange, leaf green, a pale lilac moon, an orange-striped gas giant with lilac rings, a pink ice planet with white caps. Planets are told apart by colour first, so each new one needs its own hue.
- **Star.** An unlit yellow sphere with a flat halo of a few see-through discs that always face the camera. No bloom or glow pass.
- **Rings and orbits.** Unlit, flat bands with hard edges. Every orbit is drawn as a thin, faint lilac line, so the player can see where each planet is going.
- **Planet icons** (`planet_icon.gdshader`). The UI shows a planet as a flat disc in its one icon colour, with the same purple night side as in the world, lit from the upper left. A planet with rings shows them as a line at its tilt, passing behind the disc at the top and in front at the bottom; its disc is smaller, so the rings fit at any tilt. Each planet's icon colour is set by hand to the colour it reads as from the camera.
- **Names.** Each planet and moon has its name just under it: small off-white text with the sky's lilac, a little see-through, with a thin navy outline so it still reads where it crosses an orbit line. The text faces the camera and keeps its size on screen at any zoom; the bus passes in front of it.
- **Sky** (`SpaceSky`). A deep navy-violet background with faint coloured fog and two layers of small stars. It is dim on purpose, so the planets stand out. It doesn't light the planets.

- **Bus** (`game/bus/models/bus.glb`, from the model library). A flat yellow space bus with rounded, faceted edges: a sloped windscreen, a row of dark lilac side windows over a white stripe, a door, a white roof with two vents, a dark hull underneath (no wheels) and two thrusters at the back. It is lit with the planets' two tones in the colours it was made with. The thrusters are dark while it is docked. As the drive charges, a pale cyan exhaust cone grows out of each one, full length in flight, and shrinks back after it docks. In flight the bus leaves a flat pale cyan trail on the orbit plane that narrows and fades out over 2.5 s. Under the pointer it has a white border as wide on screen as a planet's: only past its silhouette, never over its own roof, vents or nozzles (`bus_outline.gdshader`, drawn where the bus hasn't marked the stencil).

Anything new (passengers, UI) follows the same rules: flat colour, hard edges, a two-tone shade at most, no textures. It keeps the scene cheap on the web too: no post-processing so far, since each effect adds a full-screen pass (`docs/web-constraints.md`).

The bus flies on the orbit plane at 1.2 units a second, a little faster than the mockup's trip times: a planet orbits at under 40 % of the bus's speed, so the bus catches one without a long chase. Docked, it floats beside its planet (a barely visible bob and sway) and moves with it. Leaving a planet charges the drive first: a white ring round the bus fills clockwise (temporary), and the bus shakes slightly, unless Reduce motion is on. While it charges, the bus turns toward its next stop, so it leaves already facing it, and speeds up as it goes. Flying to a planet, it slows down as it nears it, then parks: it glides the last half unit in close to the planet and swings side-on, with its door toward it, so passengers get off from the side. Passengers' patience holds while it parks, and it has docked (the bell rings and the stop starts) only once it is parked. A course set while it parks charges once it has. Charging, it pulls out a little from the planet as it turns, so its tail clears it. It passes through points in space, docks at every planet on the route and charges again before leaving each one, and holds still at a point in space that ends the route. A planet stop is the planet's near side, so the bus chases a moving planet. Right-clicking the planet it is docked at adds it as the last stop, so a route can end where it started; with no route, it does nothing. Until the player first sets a course, a hint at the bottom of the screen says how: "Right-click planets or space to add stops. Backspace clears the route." The route is a flat orange line from the bus through each stop, with a diamond at each point in space. It runs to a planet's surface, and every planet on the route has an orange border, so planets read as part of the route. Under the pointer, the last stop's diamond turns white, as a planet's border does, and right-clicking it removes it. Diamonds earlier on the route stay orange.

**Opening a planet.** The camera blends in to the planet, from the same angle, and follows it along its orbit. A bigger planet fills more of the screen's height, from about a third for the Moon to half for the Giant, so sizes still compare; and the camera stays far enough back that rings fit across the space beside the panel. The rings are why the Giant can't come closer: they are 2.2 times its radius. It sits in the middle of the space left of the panel, which slides in down the right side of the screen in step with the camera, arriving as the zoom completes (`game/solar_system/planet_panel.tscn`): the planet's icon and name, a row for each passenger waiting there, longest waiting first, with their portrait, their name over their destination, and their fare, kept up to date as they arrive and board, and under them the Fuel section (Core loop, Refuelling, as built). A passenger who turns up while the panel is open slides in from its right edge; the rows already there stay as they are. The panel covers the credits, the dock panel and the bus status while open, and the course hint hides. The planets' names fade out, and the open planet's white hover border fades out and stays off while it is open, so nothing sits on the close-up; both fade back in when it closes. Pan and zoom do nothing while a planet is open; closing it blends the camera back to where it was. Right click still sets the course as usual.

**Opening the bus.** Works like opening a planet: the camera blends in to the bus and follows it, docked or in flight, framed in the space left of the bus panel (`game/bus/bus_panel.tscn`), which slides in down the right side the same way. The panel is titled "Frameshift Bus" and lists the bus's stats (Research, Stats, as built), one row each: its name, its value, and once upgrades change it, how much they add in all in green ("Top speed 1.44 +20%"), or take away in red ("Fuel tank 80 -20%"). Seats show in whole seats, and what they add as seats ("Seats 5 +1"). Under them, a Traits section names each trait the bus has ("Fuel forecast"), or reads "None yet.". Rows update as modifiers and traits come and go. Only one of the bus and a planet is open at a time.

WASD reads physical key positions, so it sits in the same place on every keyboard layout. Edge pan starts only once the pointer has moved inside the game, and stops when it leaves the window or the window loses focus, so a pointer that left through an edge doesn't keep the camera moving. In the example game scene, a left click on the board or a piece's top places a piece.

## Scenes and flow

```
main menu ──Play──▶ solar system ──Esc──▶ pause menu ──Main menu──▶ main menu
    │                                          │
    └──Settings──▶ settings panel ◀──────────Settings
```

- **Main menu** (`ui/main_menu/`): the main scene. Title, Play, Settings, Quit (Quit hidden on web). On web, a title card (game title, "Click to start") comes first, and that click starts the menu music.
- **Example game** (`game/game.tscn`): no longer reached from the menu; its tests still load it. The camera with one Phantom Camera shot (`BoardShot`), one shadowed sun, the soft-look environment, the board, and the HUD (`game/hud.tscn`) with the placed count. Placing a piece emits `Events.piece_placed`, and the HUD listens. Placing a piece calls `Feedback.shake`, which does nothing when Reduce motion is on. It instances the pause menu.
- **Pause menu** (`ui/pause_menu/`): Resume, Settings, Main menu. Pauses the tree; music keeps playing. Also opens when the player leaves browser fullscreen.
- **Settings panel** (`ui/settings/`): Master, Music and SFX volume, Reduce motion, and Edge pan (on by default; off, only the keys pan the camera). Shared by the main menu and the pause menu.
- **Solar system** (`game/solar_system/`): what Play opens. A yellow star and four flat, toon-lit planets (one with a moon, one with rings) on circular orbits, over the `SpaceSky` with parallax on (its star and fog layers drift slightly as the camera moves, and hold still under Reduce motion), seen through an RTS camera rig (`camera_rig.tscn`): one Phantom Camera shot that pans and zooms within limits set on the rig. The scene's script turns the pointer into planet hover, opening planets and course clicks for the bus (`game/bus/bus.tscn`), which starts docked at Ember. It also runs the passengers (a `Cabin`, `WaitingPassengers`, and the HUD with the passenger cards and credits). It instances the pause menu.

Scene changes go through `SceneFlow.go_to`, which fades to a black loading card ("Loading…" over a bar) while the next scene loads, then back in. The bar counts the real work in steps, so it moves on the single-threaded web build too: loading the scene's files one by one, then drawing the scene the first time one new material a frame, which compiles their shaders and is most of the wait on the web.

## Scope

- **In:** the core loop, the temporary goal, one kind of passenger, fuel and depots. Already built: the scenes above, ambient menu music, UI and placement SFX, settings saved across sessions.
- **Out for now:** everything under Research until it is designed, saving progress, dialogue, positional or adaptive audio.

## Research

Features we will very probably build, but that need more design work first.

- **Research tree.** Upgrades for the bus, bought with credits and research points. Each passenger type gives a different kind of research point.
  - **Stats, as built.** An upgrade that makes the bus better at something, rather than adding an amenity, is a modifier on one of the bus's stats (`game/bus/stat.gd`). A stat is a base value with modifiers on top, each flat or a percent: the flat ones add to the base, then the percents add together and scale the sum, so a base of 8 with +5, +10 % and +20 % is (8 + 5) × 1.3, in any order. Saves will store which upgrades were bought and apply their modifiers again on load. The stats so far: top speed (base 1.2), fuel tank (base 100, the fuel a full tank holds) and seats (base 4, rounded to the nearest whole seat). Each has a floor however much modifiers take away (0.1 top speed, 1 fuel, 1 seat), so the bus always moves, holds fuel and has a seat. A bigger tank's new room starts empty, and a smaller one spills what no longer fits. Fewer seats than riders leaves everyone on board, someone already picked up still gets on, and nobody else boards until enough have got off. Passengers' patience and fares still come from the base top speed, so a faster bus makes for happier passengers rather than less patient ones.
  - **Traits, as built.** An upgrade that unlocks a feature, rather than making a number bigger, gives the bus a trait (`game/bus/bus_traits.gd`): the bus either has it or doesn't, and the feature shows or works only while it does. Two upgrades can give the same trait; it stays until both take it back. There are two so far, and no upgrade sells either yet; the debug panel's Traits card turns each on. **Waiting markers** show how many wait at each planet, under its name (Passengers, Waiting and boarding); without them, the player finds out by opening a planet. The **fuel forecast** (`game/solar_system/fuel_forecast.tscn`): over the route's last stop, just above the planet or the diamond, how much of the tank the whole course burns ("-22% fuel"), in the planets' names' off-white, or in low fuel's red when the bus hasn't that much left. It counts the straight legs from the bus through each stop as everything is now, short of each planet by where the bus starts to park (parking burns none), so with planets moving it is an estimate. It hides with no course and during a tow, and fades with the planets' names while a planet is open.
- **Passenger types.** Around three types with different qualities. The prototype had Business (pays 1.6×, little patience), Tourist (pays 1×, lots of patience) and Student (pays 0.7×, in between). Types also differ in what they need on board, so a rich passenger might want a sleeping pod where a student wants only snacks.
- **Environment.** How space affects the bus and passengers. A hot star makes passengers on a route near it hot; a big planet's gravity slows the bus down near it.

### Open questions

- **Happiness score.** What it measures. One option: the average mood of the passengers on board, with the 20 s countdown paused when the bus is empty, so a bus full of furious passengers is the losing state.
- **Tow.** Set for now at 20 credits, or all the player has, and 0.4 units a second faster than the planet it goes to (Core loop, Fuel, as built). Tune both in playtests. The credits drop with no sign of why yet; a "-20" like the payments' gain would show it.
- **Fuel shown for a free point and for waypoints.** Whether the route line shows the total fuel and time like a planet does.
- **Passenger cards while a planet is open.** They stay down the left edge and can cover part of the open planet. Hide them, shrink them, or leave them?
- **Boarding with the docked planet open.** The planet panel covers the dock panel, and its rows have no Board button, so the player closes it to pick anyone up. Add Board buttons to the planet panel while the bus is docked there, or leave it?
- **Seats and waiting passengers.** The prototype had 4 seats and up to 3 waiting per planet; keep them until playtests say otherwise.
